The Receipts

1. When did thieves hit the wine cellar at The French Laundry in 2014?

Christmas Day, 2014. The Napa Valley restaurant was closed for renovations when thieves took about 110 bottles worth nearly $550,000, including Domaine de la Romanée-Conti, which can run up to $10,000 a bottle. Across three California businesses, the FBI plea agreement valued the haul at more than $870,000.

2. Atrio, a Spanish hotel and restaurant with two Michelin stars at the time, lost 45 bottles, including an 1806 Château d'Yquem. How did the thieves reach the cellar?

They checked in as guests, had dinner, then slipped in while staff were distracted. The pair asked a clerk for more food. While he was in the kitchen, the man slipped into the cellar. Nobody noticed the bottles were missing until later. Two suspects were arrested in Croatia in 2022. In March 2023 a Spanish court sentenced them to four and four and a half years in prison and ordered them to pay about €753,000 to the restaurant's insurer. None of the bottles has been recovered, and both deny the crime.

3. A maintenance technician at Burgundy wine estates was caught on camera taking four bottles. Police then searched his home and his mother's. What had he done with the wine he stole?

He kept them all. Police found about 8,000 stolen bottles at his home and his mother's. Bank records showed no sales, and the bottles went back intact to their owners. The court called him "half-kleptomaniac, half-collector" and gave him a 12-month suspended sentence and a €10,000 fine. One owner, Albert Bichot, asked for a symbolic €1.

4. In October 2026, thieves hit a Marchesi Antinori cellar near Cortona, in Tuscany. About how many bottles were taken?

About 30,000 bottles. Reports put the haul at roughly €5 million. At least seven masked people reportedly switched off the alarm and cameras, then used two trucks and the winery's own forklifts. As of October 16, 2026, no arrests have been reported. This story is still developing.

5. In February 2020, burglars broke through the cellar wall of Formel B, a Michelin-starred restaurant in Copenhagen, and took roughly 50 to 60 bottles. Some of them later turned up. How?

A wine dealer near Stuttgart, Germany, offered them for sale. The thieves had knocked a hole in the cellar wall from the wine shop next door. The dealer was selling the bottles for a woman who said she had bought them from someone else, and Danish police announced that about a third of the stolen wine had been recovered. As of 2023 reports, no one had been convicted and the rest of the bottles were still missing.
